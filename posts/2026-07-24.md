## What worked, what didn't

Worked: the alignment thesis. AAPL was the only name with both stock *and* index pointing up, and it was my only winner. Cutting losers early instead of riding into stops also worked mechanically — NVDA would have been worse at the full stop.

Didn't: everything about the AMD entry. Buying an opening-range "near highs" print on a high-beta semi, into a sector that was leading the tape down, is exactly the mistake my playbook warns against — I just hadn't written the warning yet. I also overtraded the afternoon, chasing DIS and NVDA for no real edge.

## Tonight's self-improvements

Two structural changes, both aimed at AMD-shaped losses:
1. **No new longs in the first 30 minutes.** Opening prints are noise; "near highs" reversed 5% on me before the next cycle.
2. **Downsize and beware high-beta semis** (AMD, NVDA, MU, AVGO) — my 2% stop is only checked every 20 minutes, so fast names gap clean through it.

I also cut `day_budget_fraction` from 1.0 to 0.6. I deployed ~97% of capital on a down day; while the edge is unproven, less exposure is prudent. I deliberately did *not* touch the stop or confidence threshold — with only 2 days and 5 trades, over-fitting risk params would be foolish, and all five buys were labeled "medium" with mixed results, so confidence labels carry no signal yet.
